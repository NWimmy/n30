Composite rating

What goes into each team's number

Every FBS team gets a composite rating built from a weighted blend of real inputs: recency-weighted SP+ (Bill Connelly's advanced efficiency rating), recruiting and transfer-portal talent, returning offensive and defensive production, head coach career win percentage, quarterback quality (real play-by-play production, not a preseason guess — see below), special-teams efficiency, and NFL draft departure impact.

Weights are not hand-picked. Each input is validated with a residual-signal test — does it predict a team's rating trajectory after controlling for every other input, or is it just correlated noise that another input already explains? Inputs that fail this test are kept in the data (so they're still visible for reference) but weighted at zero rather than removed outright. Structural constants — home-field advantage, a bye-week rest bonus, a "trap game" letdown penalty, and a per-conference strength adjustment — are fit separately against 10+ years of real FBS game results using a regularized maximum-likelihood approach (a shrinkage prior keeps a thin sample from overfitting to a fluke), not guessed.

Every backtested constant is also checked with leave-one-season-out cross-validation: refit on all but one season, then scored on the held-out season, repeated for every season in the window. A fit that only looks good in-sample and falls apart out-of-sample gets flagged rather than trusted.

Quarterback production

Real play-by-play data, not a name you'd recognize from ESPN

QB quality is built from actual per-play production data (points-per-play added), play-count-weighted so a QB's most-played games count for more than mop-up duty, with garbage-time snaps down-weighted separately. A transfer QB's production at their old school carries over with a fitted adjustment (real transfers, on average, outperform a flat continuation of their raw numbers); a genuinely unproven or backup-caliber starter gets a modest penalty instead. Every adjustment here was tested against real historical outcomes before being applied, the same way every other input is.

Season simulation

How preseason ratings turn into playoff odds

The composite rating anchors each team's starting Elo. Every scheduled FBS game for the season is simulated 10,000 times: each simulated result updates both teams' Elo (scaled by real margin of victory, not just win/loss), which then feeds into the next game on that team's schedule — so a team's playoff odds reflect its full remaining schedule's difficulty, not just its preseason number in isolation. Already-completed real games use their actual final score rather than being simulated.

Conference champions are determined by conference win percentage (Elo-tiebroken), with the top two finishers playing a simulated title game. This is a known simplification, not a claim of matching every conference's real bylaws — it doesn't model divisions/pods where a conference still uses them, or each conference's actual tiebreaker order (head-to-head, common opponents, committee ranking, etc.). The 12-team CFP field then seeds by rating: the 5 highest-rated conference champions get auto bids (4 Power Conference champions plus the highest-rated Group of 5 champion), the remaining 7 spots go to the highest-rated non-champions, and the top 4 teams in the full field get a first-round bye — matching the CFP's real 2026 format.

Market comparison

What "Mkt" columns mean, and their real limits

Market odds are hand-compiled, point-in-time sportsbook lines (FanDuel/BetMGM), devigged to strip out the book's built-in margin before comparing against this model's own fair odds. A book's full board is normalized so its listed odds sum to exactly 100%; where two books quote the same team, their two devigged numbers are averaged. These are snapshots, not a live feed — a line quoted here can move well before you'd see it reflected on this page again, and not every team is quoted by every book, so "--" means no market line was found, not a 0% model probability.

Honest caveats

What this model is and isn't

This is a preseason research model for one person's own use, published for interest, not a professional betting product. It has known simplifications (the conference-title proxy above is one; there are others) that are being actively investigated and refined, not hidden. It is not betting advice, carries no guarantee of predictive accuracy, and a favorable "edge" against a market line is not a guaranteed win rate. As the real season plays out, several inputs (SP+, QB production, Elo) update to reflect real in-season results rather than staying frozen at their preseason values.
